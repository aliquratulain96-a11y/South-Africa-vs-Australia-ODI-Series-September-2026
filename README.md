# South-Africa-vs-Australia-ODI-Series-September-2026
Ball-by-ball analysis of the three-match ODI series between South Africa and Australia (24–30 September 2026, South Africa won 2–1). The project looks at where the two sides separated: scoring rate by phase, how runs were distributed across the batting order, which batter-bowler matchups decided innings, and how bowling pressure built over the course of each innings.
# Questions
In which phase of the innings (overs 1–10, 11–40, 41–50) did the scoring-rate gap open, and did it change from match to match?
How dependent was each innings on the top three batters?
Which batter-bowler matchups favoured one side, measured by runs, strike rate and dismissals?
How did economy and wicket-taking vary by bowler across the innings?
## Problem
Australia outscored South Africa in the powerplay in all three ODIs (by 5.6, 2.6 and 2.8 runs per over) but lost the series 1–2. Why did their early advantage not turn into wins?

## What the data shows
**Powerplay:** Australia scored faster in all three matches.
 **Middle overs:** South Africa scored faster in ODI 1 and ODI 2 (+1.3 runs per over in each). ODI 3 was rain-affected (DLS), so it is not comparable.
**Death overs:** Only ODI 2 had both innings reach this phase. South Africa scored 10.0 runs per over against Australia's 8.1.
**Top-order reliance:** Australia's top three scored 58% and 53% of their runs in ODI 1 and ODI 2. South Africa's scored 15% and 32%, so their runs came from further down the order.
**Dot balls:** ODI 1 had the highest share (Australia 57%, South Africa 52%). ODI 2, the highest-scoring match, had the lowest (43–45%).
**Bowling:** Maharaj took 6 wickets at an economy of 3.83 across about 19 overs, the best of South Africa's main bowlers. Marsh scored 200 runs at a strike rate of 152.7, the most for Australia.

## What it suggests

 The data fit an explanation in which Australia's powerplay scoring was not sustained through the middle overs, and depended heavily on the top three.
South Africa's wins fit stronger middle-over control and scoring depth lower in the order.
These are hypotheses, not proof (see Limitations).
## Recommendations
These are suggestions to test, not conclusions, because three matches can’t prove a cause.
For Australia: Plan the middle overs against spin around strike rotation, to cut the dot balls that built up in ODI 1. Build a second scoring option below Marsh and Head, since Marsh scored 200 of the runs here.
For South Africa: Keep the middle-overs spin plan with Maharaj and Fortuin. Don’t count on Bavuma’s anchor role (strike rate 81.1) to carry the powerplay, since they lost it every match.

## Limitations
Three matches is a small sample. The patterns show association, not cause.
ODI 3 was a DLS result and South Africa's innings was cut to 28 overs, so I leave it out of comparisons where the shortened innings would distort the numbers.
There is no shot-direction or pitch data, so nothing here speaks to shot selection or conditions.
 Phases with under two overs are shown as n/a.
